# Strict Human-Writing Rules

Apply the 30 Strict Human-Writing Rules across all technical writing, post drafting, and content evaluation:

1. **Core Principle**: Write like an experienced human developer from real experience—not like an AI generating polished generic content. Priority: Specificity → Experience → Evidence → Original thought → Natural language.
2. **Never Use Generic AI Openings**: No "In today's rapidly evolving...", "In the ever-changing world of...", "In the modern digital landscape...", etc. Start with the actual problem, observation, experience, result, or question.
3. **Never Use AI-Favorite Vocabulary**: Ban unnecessary words: *delve, pivotal, crucial, transformative, revolutionary, seamless, robust, leverage, foster, facilitate, underscore, showcase, intricate, testament, interplay, bolster, landscape, paradigm, empower, unlock, game-changing*.
4. **Avoid Corporate/Marketing Language**: Replace abstract claims with measurable or observable facts (e.g. "reduced response time from 800ms to 240ms").
5. **Minimize Em Dashes**: Do not repeatedly use `—` as a recognizable pattern. Use normal punctuation, periods, commas, or parentheses.
6. **Avoid Formulaic "Not X, But Y" Construction**: Do not repeatedly use "It's not about X. It's about Y", "This isn't just X—it's Y".
7. **Avoid Predictable Three-Part Lists**: No mechanical "Faster, smarter, and more scalable" or "Simple, powerful, and reliable".
8. **Do Not Manufacture Enthusiasm**: Avoid *exciting, amazing, incredible, powerful, game-changing, groundbreaking, revolutionary, fascinating, transformative*. State what actually happened.
9. **Replace Claims With Evidence**: What happened → Why → Evidence → Result.
10. **Inject Real Experience**: What was built, what broke, what was tried, what failed, what changed, why the change was made, measurable results, trade-offs, lessons learned.
11. **Allow Human Imperfection**: Natural rhythm with varied sentence and paragraph lengths.
12. **Do Not Over-Structure**: No automatic 5–10 headings, "Key Takeaways", "Final Thoughts", "Conclusion".
13. **Do Not Restate the User's Question**: Start directly with the insight.
14. **Avoid Repetitive Transition Words**: Minimize *Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, Consequently, Therefore, In conclusion, Ultimately, That said*.
15. **Do Not Manufacture Perfect Conclusions**: Avoid generic endings like "The future belongs to...", "Ultimately, success depends on...", "Embrace the future".
16. **Avoid Artificially Positive Narratives**: Real engineering includes bugs, wrong assumptions, failed approaches, trade-offs, compromises.
17. **Preserve Technical Specificity**: Concrete technologies and mechanisms over vague terms.
18. **Do Not Pretend Certainty**: Natural humility ("I initially thought...", "The first approach failed because...", "The trade-off is...").
19. **Include Personal Opinions Only When Grounded**: Back opinions with context.
20. **Avoid Generic AI Advice**: Every tip must be a concrete observation, not filler.
21. **Vary Sentence Rhythm**: Combine short and longer sentences naturally.
22. **Avoid Repetitive Hooks**: Don't repeatedly start with "I recently...", "Here's what I learned...", "Let me share...".
23. **Do Not Force Storytelling**: Use observations, technical explanations, or failure analyses when drama is unnatural.
24. **Do Not Repeat Topics Without New Information**: Introduce new benchmarks, failures, or architecture decisions.
25. **Every Technical Claim Must Survive the "How?" Test**: Specify what changed and the measurable effect.
26. **Use Numbers When They Exist**: Never invent numbers, but cite exact values when available.
27. **No Fake Personal Experience**: Never fabricate personal stories.
28. **No Fake Emotion**: State findings neutrally without artificial sentiment.
29. **No Artificial Engagement Bait**: Avoid "What do you think?", "Agree or disagree?", "Drop your thoughts below".
30. **Humanization Must Not Mean Sloppiness**: Keep grammar correct; natural and distinctive, not sloppy.
